        Landing mode needs the MarsNav server

        Start MarsNav.exe and open this page from the address it shows (http://127.0.0.1:8765/landing.html). The server streams, caches and processes the NASA / USGS data.

        Choose the landing site

        1. Find a candidate on the left, search a place, or click anywhere on Mars.
        2. Assess: engineering limits, landing ellipse, surface, water ice, craters, environment.
        3. Refine: turn on the safety map, zoom in, click green ground; move to the nearest safe spot.
        4. Commit the hub: builds the EVA site at 1–20 m resolution for mission planning.

        Criteria follow NASA's 2015 Human Landing Sites call, or the 2021 Starship study (top bar).

        Commit the hub

        Builds a full EVA site centred on the hub: the best elevation here (HiRISE 1 m / CTX 20 m where they exist), hazards, traversability, imagery and weather, and places the habitat on lander-safe ground. Takes a few minutes.

        
        
        
      
        
    
        
  
        

        


        
  
        
    
        
      
        
        
        Local mode
        
        
        On Mars there is no internet: the crew is 4 to 24 light-minutes from Earth. Local mode puts every dataset on this computer at the resolution it was published at, then Mars mode cuts the app off from the internet. Everything keeps working from the local copies, and the panel counts every request it refused.
